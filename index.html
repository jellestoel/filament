How to read the numbers

  • The list assumes a printer like the Bambu Lab X2D, with a 300 °C nozzle, 120 °C bed, and 65 °C heated chamber.
  • Stiffness is XY bending modulus, and strength is XY bending strength.
  • Toughness is XY impact strength, and layer adhesion is Z impact strength.
  • Heat is HDT at 0.45 MPa, a light load. A material only passes if its HDT is at least 10 °C above your temperature, so heavily loaded parts need more margin.
  • Compared to PLA divides stiffness, strength, toughness, and layer adhesion by PLA Basic's values, so 2.0× means double. Bars don't change. Other columns keep their normal units.
  • Bars in a column share one scale, starting at zero, so lengths compare directly. Toughness and layer bars widen their scale when TPU is shown, and a note says when.
  • Great for lines are examples, not approval of a specific part. Test anything load-bearing or safety-related.
  • Passing only means a material matches the rules set here. It doesn't prove a real part will work.
  • PET-CF, PAHT-CF, and PA6-CF heat values come from annealed test bars. Bambu suggests annealing printed parts at about 80 to 130 °C for 5 to 12 hours, so an unannealed part may soften earlier. Bambu doesn't publish how much earlier.
  • Values come from Bambu Lab test bars at 100% infill. Real parts, and other brands, will differ.
  • Bambu's newer PETG Basic sheet lists higher strength (81 MPa) and stiffness (1790 MPa) than its guide. This page uses the guide so the materials stay comparable.
  • Spring back is my judgment for clips and snap fits, based on general material behavior. Stretch before break alone doesn't show that a part springs back, so test your part, because repeated bending can still fatigue it.
  • Stretch before break comes from Bambu's data sheets, and TPU bars are cut off because they pass 650%.
  • Ease, sag resistance, accuracy and fit, warp resistance, and surface are my 1 to 5 estimates from general material behavior on an enclosed printer, where 5 is best. They aren't lab data, so print a test for parts that must fit.
  • Fume risk is my rough 1 to 5 estimate of print emissions, where 1 is lowest. It leans on limited studies and base polymers, and it isn't a medical or safety rating. Brand, color, and nozzle temperature all change emissions.
  • UV resistance is a 1 to 5 estimate from outdoor material guides. In the sun needs 5, and partial shade needs at least 3. With no sun exposure selected, UV rules nothing out. Color, grade, and climate all change the result, and stabilized PC or nylon can last much longer.
  • Overhang and bridging are the limits listed on Bambu's data sheets, not a side-by-side test, so small differences may not hold on real prints.
  • Cost bands are rounded euros per kg before shipping, for a single 1 kg roll without sales or bulk discounts. They were checked against regular EU shop prices in November 2026. Plain TPU 95A uses the 95A HF price as a proxy. TPU for AMS prices varied a lot, so its band is a middle estimate. PLA Silk Dual is also an estimate, because the only price found came from a shop that ran high on other filaments. Prices vary by shop.
  • Bed ranges are for textured PEI on base grades. PLA variants use the range from their own data sheets.
  • Color can shift these numbers, especially on specialty colors, so treat each row as typical for that product.
  • After getting wet notes are general guidance, except the nylon warning, which comes from Bambu's drying guide.
  • Air quality notes are qualitative, because no single study covers all of these materials.
